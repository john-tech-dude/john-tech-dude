巻物

穂波 — HONAMI

一
The Night at Honami

Honami (穂波) — "waves of rice ears" — was a village of granaries and drying racks, where the ripe rice-heads moved in the wind like water. The Kage-Nezumi came for the rice and stayed to burn.

The Black Crescent took the granaries first. They took Nazumi's parents second. He was small enough to hide in the temple rafters. A blade does not look up for what it cannot see. He watched through the smoke and made no sound, and that is the only reason he lived.

一

Some ash does not wash off, pup.

Takashi
二
The Soldier Who Stayed

A soldier climbed into the rafters. The Kage-Nezumi crest was on his sleeve and his hands were still wet from the night's work. Nazumi waited for the cut.

The soldier looked at him for a long time. Then he sheathed his sword and held out a paw.

"I will not name what I owe you, pup. But I will not leave you to die in our ashes."

His name was Takashi.

隆 — TAKASHI

三
The Wooden Needle

At dawn, in the burned temple, Takashi carved a needle of wood from a splinter of charred beam. He closed the pup's fingers around it and aimed it at the smoke still lifting from the granaries.

Then he turned the point down, into the dust between them.

"The weight comes before the cut. A sword is neither good nor evil. Like its wielder, it simply is. The question is always the wielder."
四
The Ash Road

They left Honami together because Nazumi had nowhere else to go. The road ran through Iwane, the Stone-Root villages, past cracked millstones and burrows sealed from the inside. It was not kind to the small.

Nazumi kept looking back at the thinning smoke. Takashi never once did. He had done enough looking back.

Takashi never spoke of Honami. Nazumi never asked about the wet on his hands. Each carried something the other could see and neither would name.

木霊 — KODAMA

五
The Mountain Temple

High in the peaks, where the Kage-Nezumi did not go, Takashi found an old temple. It was home until the winters turned. He taught the needle-blade the way a monk teaches silence.

Over the winters the past came out in pieces. Takashi had been a scholar of Iwane before the Black Crescent took the rice lands and took him with them.

六
The Broken Blade

In the tenth winter, a wild rat came down from the pass and challenged the temple. The duel was short. Nazumi's wooden needle snapped in the first exchange.

He stayed in the dust. He did not flail or beg. He watched, until he saw the one opening the rat had not meant to leave. Then he stood.

Walking back to the temple, holding the splintered wood, he understood that it had been teaching him all along.

THE WORLD

岩根
Iwane — Stone-Root

Iwane (岩根) — "rock root" — a real place and surname. The Stone-Root villages dot the landscape, past cracked millstones and burrows sealed from the inside.

It was not kind to the small.

影鼠
Kage-Nezumi — The Black Crescent

Kage-Nezumi (影鼠) — "shadow-rat" — the Black Crescent, the clan that takes by night. They came for the rice and stayed to burn.

Epithet: Kuro-Mikazuki (黒三日月) — the Black Crescent Moon.

戦
Clan Wars

The clan wars raged across Iwane. The Kage-Nezumi took villages for sport. Takashi had been a scholar of Iwane before the Black Crescent took the rice lands and took him with them.

He had joined them believing they would bring order. Then he watched them burn villages for sport.

THE RONIN CODE

義
義 — Righteousness

Do what is right, even when it costs you. A blade doesn't decide anything. The paw does.

誠
誠 — Sincerity

No unnamed debts. No business before dawn. The clan walks in the open.

仁
仁 — Benevolence

Protect the small. Mercy is harder than the cut. Choose it anyway.

礼
礼 — Respect

The monastery took him in without asking what he had done. The clan does the same. Everyone here is still walking.

勇
勇 — Courage

Lowering the blade is the brave act. Standing down when you could strike is harder than fighting.
